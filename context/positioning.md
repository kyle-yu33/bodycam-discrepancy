# Market Positioning and JusticeText Research

## Honest market reality

EvidenceLens is not entering an empty category. Legal-tech products already help lawyers review audiovisual evidence, transcribe video, search discovery, build timelines, organize case material, and investigate possible inconsistencies.

This validates the problem. It also means we must avoid novelty claims that cannot be supported.

## JusticeText: verified public positioning

JusticeText’s public materials describe:

- bodycam/dashcam and other audiovisual-evidence review;
- searchable, time-synced AI transcripts;
- document/PDF analysis;
- document–video cross-referencing;
- case timelines, summaries, key moments, and “spot inconsistencies” assistance;
- multi-camera synchronization, clipping, annotation, and redaction;
- defence/public-defender-oriented workflows.

Its public messaging strongly emphasizes **transcription, transcript search, document analysis, timelines, and evidence workflow**.

## What we can and cannot say

| Do not say | Defensible statement |
|---|---|
| “JusticeText does not use vision AI.” | “We found no public documentation that JusticeText uses vision AI for frame-level verification of physical claims.” |
| “JusticeText cannot analyze bodycam footage.” | “JusticeText clearly supports bodycam review; its public AI story is primarily transcript/audio-first and evidence-workflow focused.” |
| “Nobody compares reports with video.” | “EvidenceLens focuses on an explicit claim-to-evidence ledger for visually assessable report claims.” |
| “Our AI detects lies.” | “EvidenceLens surfaces source-linked visual review questions for a qualified human.” |

Missing public documentation is not proof of a company’s internal architecture. Treat the wording above as a research-bounded public claim.

## EvidenceLens wedge

> **JusticeText is a broad evidence-review platform. EvidenceLens is a focused, visual claim-audit workflow.**

The intended distinction is not merely “we use AI on video.” It is:

1. a written narrative is decomposed into atomic claims;
2. each claim is classified by whether video can reasonably assess it;
3. every automated suggestion carries report wording, timestamps, source frames, observations, and limitations;
4. the system can abstain instead of treating missing footage as contradiction;
5. a qualified human makes the legal conclusion.

## Competitive posture for the pitch

Do not attack JusticeText. Acknowledge that current products prove the evidence-review pain is real.

Say:

> “Current legal-tech tools help teams search, transcribe, organize, and investigate evidence. EvidenceLens focuses on a narrower trust problem: making each concrete report claim traceable to the visual evidence that supports, challenges, or cannot assess it.”

## Real-world thesis

Vision-capable models can already inspect video, but they remain unreliable for fine-grained, fast, occluded, ambiguous, or legally interpretive situations. A real product must therefore be human-supervised, privacy-conscious, source-grounded, and auditable.

The long-term opportunity is not autonomous legal truth detection. It is a progressively stronger evidence-review layer as video understanding improves.
